# PORTFOLIO MANAGER REVIEW
### Would I want this on my desk every earnings season?
**Written from the seat of:** Head PM, event-driven fund · **Date:** 2026-07-03
**Lens:** my comp depends only on long-term P&L. Engineering is solved; I don't care about it.

> **One-line answer:** Yes to a *30-second quality-of-beat card*, no to the current
> 11-section report. It earns a permanent triage slot for two moments — building the
> pre-earnings scrutiny list and gut-checking "is this beat real" on names outside my
> core coverage. It does **not** belong in my trade sizing (wrong horizon), and it has
> one disqualifying gap for a *yes without conditions*: **it measures quality-of-earnings
> but not surprise-versus-expectations, and event-driven money is made on the latter.**

---

## The uncomfortable framing problem (read this first)

I run *event-driven* money. I get paid on what a stock does in the minutes-to-weeks
around a catalyst. This engine's validated evidence is **3/6/12-month** forward returns
and **quarterly** accounting drift. That is a slow signal handed to a fast desk.

That mismatch does not kill it — but it relocates its value. This is **not** a
signal I trade. It is a **triage filter** that tells me *where to point my scarce
attention* before and after a print. Used that way, it's useful. Used as a trade
trigger, it will get me run over. The rest of this memo assumes the triage framing.

---

## Project 1 — Pre-earnings workflow, and where the engine belongs

My actual sequence the week before a cluster of prints:

1. **Map the calendar** — who reports when, where I have position or interest.
   → *Engine: not here.*
2. **Set expectations** — consensus, buy-side whisper, my model, options-implied move,
   positioning/crowding. This is the core of the trade.
   → *Engine: NOT here, and this is its biggest blind spot. It has no estimates, no
   whisper, no implied move. It cannot tell me what's priced.*
3. **Build the surprise thesis** — what could beat/miss, and where might the reported
   number be lower-quality than it looks.
   → **Engine belongs HERE, and only here, pre-print.** The weekend before, run it across
   the whole reporting calendar and get a ranked list of names whose *reported quality has
   been drifting*. That tells me which "clean beats" to distrust on Thursday morning and
   which prints deserve my sharpest read. This is genuinely useful: it pre-loads suspicion.
4. **Pre-position or decide to trade the reaction** — sizing, hedges, options structure.
   → *Engine: not here. Wrong horizon; would corrupt sizing.*

**Verdict P1:** the engine is a *stage-3 scrutiny-router*, not an expectations or sizing
input. Its pre-earnings job is one sentence: *"of the 30 names printing this week, these
5 have deteriorating earnings quality — read those hardest."*

## Project 2 — Post-earnings workflow, and how the engine changes it

The print hits. My sequence, in the first 15 minutes:

1. Headline beat/miss vs consensus. 2. The **guide** (usually matters more than the print).
3. Reaction vs implied move. 4. Decision: fade / chase / hold / flip / do nothing.

**This is the engine's single best moment** — the *quality-of-beat* question inside step 1.
A stock beats EPS, pops 9%, and my only question is *fade or chase*. If the engine can tell
me in 30 seconds *"the beat did not come with cash — CFO/NI collapsed, receivables spiked
against a 'strong demand' script,"* that materially sharpens the fade case. That is the
engineered-beat detection, delivered in the window where it's most decision-relevant.

But: it must arrive in **under 5 minutes and one screen**, because on a heavy morning I
have 20 names printing and ~90 seconds each. The current report cannot be read in that
window. So the engine's best moment is currently *unusable* purely because of packaging.

**Verdict P2:** post-print, the engine changes my process from "eyeball the cash flow
statement myself if I have time" (I usually don't, in the moment) to "instant quality-of-
beat read." High potential value, blocked entirely by report length.

## Project 3 — Section-by-section, would I read it?

Ranked by what a PM actually does with it during earnings season.

| Section | Read it? | Trust? | Changes priorities? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Metric/Narrative Mismatches** | **Yes** | Yes* | **Yes** | **Keep — lead with it.** The conflicts (demand vs working capital, profitability vs cash) *are* the "what do I investigate" answer. |
| **What Changed This Period** | **Yes** | Yes | Yes | **Keep — #2.** Deltas are what a PM lives on; trajectory > level. |
| **Analyst Review Questions** | Yes | n/a | Yes | **Keep — this is literally "what to investigate next."** Rename to that. |
| **Red Flags** (top 3, ranked) | Skim | Yes* | Sometimes | **Keep top 3 only.** More than 3 and I stop reading. |
| **Overall + block scores** | Glance | As a *filter*, not a verdict | As triage yes | **Keep as a triage gate** ("is this name worth 20 min?"), demote as a verdict. |
| **Narrative Drift Summary** | Skim | Partially (uncalibrated) | Occasionally | Collapse to 2 lines. Useful color, secondary. |
| Evidence Ledger | **No** (until I disagree) | Yes | No | **Move to appendix / drill-down.** I don't read it; I *cite* it when I want to argue with the flag. Must exist, must not be in my face. |
| Green Flags | **No** | n/a | No | **Delete / one line.** I'm paid to find risk; reassurance is my default assumption. |
| Metric Detail / Data Quality | No | The *data-unavailable* flag matters | No | **Appendix** — but the "unavailable ≠ all-clear" distinction must survive so I don't misread a coverage gap as a green light. |
| Disclaimer | **Ignore** | n/a | No | Footer. Legal, not investment. |

**The brutal cut:** of 11 sections, **3 carry the decision value** (Mismatches, What
Changed, Investigate-Next). Everything else is either a triage gate (scores), a
drill-down (evidence, metric detail), or noise (green flags, disclaimer).

## Project 4 — Turn the accounting report into an investment research report

Same math, re-presented to answer *"what should I investigate next?"* New order:

1. **VERDICT (one line):** clean beat / dirty beat / mixed / can't tell — plus the single
   most important conflict.
2. **What changed that matters** (max 3 deltas, in plain English).
3. **Investigate next** (max 3, ranked, phrased as questions I can hand an analyst).
4. **Watch next quarter** (1–2 items).
5. **Trust** (data coverage + calibration honesty in one line).
6. *(below the fold)* everything else — evidence ledger, full metrics, scores.

**Language shift, concretely:**
- Was: `cfo_to_net_income = 0.05 (concern 94/100)`
- Now: **"The beat did not come with cash — operating cash was ~5% of reported net income
  vs a ~1.0x norm. Investigate the accrual driving it."**

Numbers unchanged. What changed is that a PM can act on it in one read.

## Project 5 — The 25-report blind test (intellectual-honesty note)

I will **not fabricate** 25 blind reads and pretend they're evidence — that is exactly the
cherry-picking the brief forbids, and inventing 25 "I would have thought X" trials would be
the least honest thing in this whole project. Here is what I can *honestly* say from evidence
that actually exists, plus the real protocol (folded into Project 9):

- **From the miss test (4/5 known blowups elevated pre-event):** on the genuinely bad names,
  the report *would* have raised a real, specific flag before the blow-up — mostly via cash
  conversion and revenue quality, the blocks calibration independently trusts. That's a real
  point in its favor.
- **From the wide sweep (13.5% of large-caps flag ≥ threshold):** "elevated" is **not rare**.
  Blind, the report would have sent me to investigate a benign name a large fraction of the
  time — consistent with the measured 44.5% false-positive rate. As a *trade signal* that's
  disqualifying; as a *triage router* it's tolerable, because triage is allowed to over-refer.
- **Net honest read:** the report would change my *research priorities* meaningfully and my
  *conviction* occasionally, but it would **not** reliably change *position sizing* — because
  a tool that flags 1-in-7 names and is wrong ~44% of the time when it flags cannot be a
  sizing input. Sizing needs precision this doesn't have. Research-routing needs recall,
  which it has.

The real 25-trial test has not been run in decision terms. That is the gap, and Project 9
is how to close it without fooling myself.

## Project 6 — The "Trading Implications" section (drafted, never says buy/sell)

Template, to sit as section 3 of the redesigned report:

```
INVESTIGATE NEXT
  1. [highest-conflict item, as a question]
     e.g. "Demand is described as strong; why did receivables grow 3x revenue? Channel,
      terms, or collection?"
  2. [second item] ...
  3. [third item] ...

TREAT WITH SKEPTICISM
  - [any headline metric the engine thinks is cosmetically supported]
     e.g. "Reported margin expansion is not confirmed by cash conversion."

CONFLICTING SIGNALS
  - [metric A] says X while [metric B] says Y — reconcile before trusting the print.

CHALLENGE THESE ASSUMPTIONS
  - [management framing] vs [what the numbers show]

MONITOR NEXT QUARTER
  - [the one trend whose continuation confirms or kills the concern]
```

Every line is a *question or a watch item*, never a directive. This is the section that
turns the engine from "accounting scorecard" into "research agenda."

## Project 7 — Analyst time saved (honest estimate)

The mechanical work the engine replaces, for **one name I don't already cover**:

| Task | Manual | With engine |
|---|---|---|
| Pull & normalize 8Q of cash flow vs earnings | 20–30 min | ~0 |
| Working-capital / receivables-vs-revenue trend | 15–20 min | ~0 |
| SBC/dilution/buyback-offset build | 10–15 min | ~0 |
| Cross-check management language vs the numbers | 20–40 min | ~0 (mismatch section) |
| **Total first-pass quality read** | **~65–105 min** | **~2 min to read the card** |

**The critical caveat: time saved is inversely proportional to how well I already know the
name.** On my 15 core positions I know the cash-conversion history cold — savings ≈ zero.
On the 300 names I *don't* cover but that print into my universe — savings ≈ an hour each,
and I'd never do that work manually, so the engine isn't saving time, it's **extending
coverage into names I'd otherwise skip entirely.** That is the bigger prize than time:
*breadth I could not otherwise afford.*

Biggest productivity gain: **the mismatch check** — cross-referencing narrative against
numbers is the most tedious manual step and the one the engine does best.

## Project 8 — Where it genuinely beats the alternatives

| Alternative | Has | Lacks vs engine | Where engine wins |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Read the 10-Q myself** | Full nuance | Time; consistency; can't do 300 names | Speed + consistency across breadth |
| **Bloomberg/FactSet** | The data, **estimates, consensus, implied move** | Synthesized quality-of-earnings read; narrative-vs-numbers check | The *synthesis* + mismatch; NOT the data or estimates |
| **Basic LLM summary** | What management *said*, fast | Checking management against the *numbers* | The engine's whole point: it doesn't summarize the story, it **audits** it |
| **Sell-side quality-of-earnings note** | Depth | Speed; coverage; timeliness; independence | Instant, every name, no waiting for an analyst |

**The one genuine, defensible time-save:** *fast, consistent, cross-name auditing of
management narrative against the reported numbers* — the mismatch detection. No single tool
on my desk does that in one place at earnings speed. Bloomberg has the numbers; the LLM has
the story; **only this engine collides them and tells me where they disagree.** That is the
sentence that justifies a desk slot.

## Project 9 — Six-month personal validation experiment (no hindsight, no cherry-picking)

**Objective:** does using the engine change my *outcomes*, or just my *comfort*?

**Design:**
- **Universe pre-committed** on day 1: my actual watchlist + every name that prints into it.
  No adding/dropping names based on results.
- **Two-envelope protocol per print:** before opening the engine, log my read — thesis,
  conviction (1–5), intended action, size. *Then* open the engine, log whether it (a) changed
  my action, (b) changed my size, (c) changed my conviction, (d) surfaced something I'd have
  missed, or (e) did nothing. Timestamp both so I can't back-fill.
- **Track outcomes** at +1 week, +1 month, +1 quarter (event-driven horizons, not the
  engine's 12-month horizon — I'm testing *my* use, not the engine's backtest).
- **Two implicit arms:** decisions where the engine changed something vs where it didn't.
  Compare hit rate and P&L of "engine-influenced" vs "engine-ignored" decisions.

**The one honest success metric that can't be gamed:** at month 6, *do I still open it
without being told to?* If a tool that costs attention isn't worth its attention cost, I
stop using it the moment no one's watching. Voluntary continued use **is** the verdict.

**Kill criteria (pre-committed):** if at 6 months the engine changed <3 decisions materially
and the engine-influenced bucket didn't outperform, I retire it to a background screen and
stop reading reports. No sunk-cost extensions.

## The verdict: would I want this on my desk every earnings season?

**Yes — conditionally, and the conditions are about packaging and one missing input, not
about the underlying work.**

**Why yes:**
- It does one thing no other tool on my desk does at earnings speed: **collide the narrative
  against the numbers and tell me where management's story and its cash disagree.**
- The miss test says that when it matters most — a name heading for trouble — it tends to
  raise a real, specific flag beforehand, driven by the metrics I'd trust anyway.
- It extends my *coverage* into hundreds of names I'd otherwise skip, which is worth more
  than the time it saves on names I know.
- As a *triage router* the 44.5% false-positive rate is acceptable — triage is allowed to
  over-refer; I'm the filter after it.

**What's missing for an unconditional yes (in priority order):**
1. **Expectations context.** The engine knows the *quality* of the number but not whether it
   *beat*. Event-driven P&L is surprise-vs-expectations. Without consensus/guide/implied-move
   sitting *next to* the quality read, I'm holding half the trade. This is the #1 gap. *(It
   doesn't need to compute estimates — it needs to not pretend the quality read is a
   complete picture, and ideally display alongside my existing consensus feed.)*
2. **The 30-second card (Project 10).** The value exists today and is 100% blocked by report
   length. This is pure packaging and it's the highest-leverage change available.
3. **Proof it changes my outcomes** — the Project 9 experiment. Until that runs, "useful" is
   a hypothesis, not a finding.

**Where it must never go:** into my sizing or my trade trigger. Wrong horizon, wrong
precision. The day someone sizes a position off this score is the day it starts losing money.

**Bottom line for the man whose comp is the P&L:** I'd take the card, not the report. I'd use
it to decide *where to look*, never *what to do*. That's a real slot on a real desk — smaller
than the project hoped, but earned, and honestly the correct size.
